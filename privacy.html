Privacy

Tapestry's privacy policy. Last changed 29 October 2026.

Tapestry is a place to keep your life's memories, events and periods, and to share them with the people you choose. This page says what Tapestry keeps about you, why, who sees it, how long it is kept, and how to have it all deleted. If you are not on Tapestry but someone named you on it, this part is for you.

Who is responsible for your data

The controller of your personal data is [legal name], trading as Latent Sea, NIF [NIF], [postal address], Spain. Write to [contact email] about anything on this page, in English or Spanish.

What Tapestry keeps

  • Your account: the name and email address of the Google account you sign in with, the name you choose to go by on Tapestry, when you joined and by whose invitation, and that you confirmed you are 14 or over.
  • What you put on it: your memories (their words, pictures, captions and links), events, periods, places and their addresses (and a point on the map, if you put one), labels, comments, likes, the people you name - with their birth and death dates, if you give them - and your circles of them.
  • Your settings: your birthday if you give it (seen by you alone, unless you choose to share its year or the whole date with your people), who sees new things by default, your kinds of period and of event, what cards show, the people you block, whether notices are emailed to you, and whom you let record your death.
  • What happens between members: invitations, requests and their answers, approvals, notices, feedback you send, and reports of content made to Tapestry's moderators.
  • Your devices: if you turn on notifications, the address your browser's push service gave this device to send them to.
  • Your plan: whether you are on the free or the paid plan and, if you pay, a reference to the payment from the store you paid through. Tapestry never sees or keeps your card details.
  • On your device only: a few choices kept in your browser, such as text size and the order you read your life in, and your sign-in, so you stay signed in. None of them is a tracking cookie, and none is sent anywhere.

Tapestry has no advertising, sells nothing about you, and uses no tracking or analytics services.

What you write may say things about your health, beliefs, relationships or other sensitive matters. When it is about you, it is kept because you chose to put it on Tapestry (your explicit consent, given on joining, which you can take back by deleting it). Please don't put such things about other people on Tapestry without their agreement: the terms don't allow it.

Why it is kept

  • To run Tapestry for you - your account, what you put on it, and showing it to whom you choose: because you asked us to (the contract between us, by the terms).
  • The people in your memories - names, dates and pictures of others you put on Tapestry: our legitimate interest, and yours, in keeping a life story that has other people in it, weighed against theirs - which is why they are seen only by whom you choose, can ask to be taken off, and anyone can report what goes too far.
  • To keep Tapestry safe and working - dealing with reports, blocking, limits on how much is sent at once, keeping a record of who looked at what, support seeing an account masked, and backups: our legitimate interest in a safe, working service, and the law's requirements of us.
  • Support seeing your account as it is: only when you let them, from You, for 24 hours at a time (your consent, which you can stop at any moment).
  • Notifications on your phone or by email: only what is new for you on Tapestry, never advertising; turned off in You at any time.

Who sees it

You decide, on each memory, event and period: only you, the people who were there, all your people, everyone on Tapestry, or only those you pick - and you can let in or keep out anyone. The platform enforces this itself, not the app on your phone. What you share with everyone on Tapestry can be found by any member with Find on Tapestry. Places are seen only through something at them; anyone but you sees only a place's town, region and country, never its street, postcode or point on the map. Your pictures are kept privately and shown only through short-lived addresses made for someone allowed to see them.

Nothing anyone else does puts your things on their events or periods, or theirs on your life, without a yes: what is put into an event or period waits for whoever runs it to approve it, and an event or period someone puts you in waits for yours.

Tapestry's admins can see who is on Tapestry and their email addresses, and the invitations waiting.

Moderators - the admins, and people they choose - see in full, pictures included, what someone reported as breaking the terms or the law, while it is reported, so they can decide whether to remove it.

Support (an admin) can see an account as its member sees it, to help with a problem - but with every word jumbled, every picture hidden and every point on a map taken off, so nothing anyone wrote or pictured can be read. Only if you let them, from You, do they see it as it is, for 24 hours at a time. Neither ever changes anything.

Every time support or a moderator sees something, it is written down: who, what and when. You see in You each time it was something of yours.

If you choose people to record your death, they can say when you died; your life on the tapestry then ends there.

Who else handles it

  • Latent Sea's own server holds everything on Tapestry - your account, what you put on it, your pictures. It is a server Latent Sea runs itself, rented from netcup GmbH in Nuremberg, Germany, inside the EU, under a data processing agreement; netcup provides the machine and never looks at what is on it.
  • Cloudflare (Cloudflare, Inc., in the United States, under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and a data processing agreement) carries everything between your device and the server, encrypted, and protects the server from attacks; it sees your device's address. Encrypted copies of the server's data (backups) are kept in Cloudflare's storage in the EU.
  • Google signs you in. Google's own privacy policy covers your Google account. Google is in the United States, under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.
  • Latent Sea's sign-in page, where you press to sign in with Google, and the app's own pages and code are served by GitHub (in the United States, under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework) - none of your data - and so GitHub sees your device's address as you open them.
  • Your browser's push service - Google's for Chrome and Android, Mozilla's for Firefox, Apple's for Safari and iPhones, Microsoft's for Edge on Windows - delivers notifications to your device if you turn them on. What it carries is encrypted, so it can't read it; it sees that a notification was sent to your device.
  • Resend (in the United States, under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and a data processing agreement) will send the emails about what is new for you, once Tapestry sends email: your email address and the notice.
  • OpenStreetMap (the OpenStreetMap Foundation, in the United Kingdom, under the EU's adequacy decision) draws the map on a place's page, and finds a place on it when you press "Find on the map": it sees your device's address, and what you typed of the place to find it. A point you put on the map is kept on Tapestry, seen by you alone.
  • YouTube (Google): a video linked on a memory shows its picture and plays only when you press it - only then does YouTube see your device's address, through its privacy-enhanced address (youtube-nocookie.com).
  • Payments, when you go on the paid plan, are taken by Google Play, under its own privacy policy.

Everything travels to and from Tapestry encrypted (HTTPS).

Deleting your account

You can delete your account and everything on it at any time, yourself: sign in, open You, and press Delete my account. Your memories, pictures, events, periods, places, people, comments, likes, notices, feedback and settings are deleted straight away, and so is your sign-in - your Google account's name and email - unless you use another of Latent Sea's apps with it. Picture files are taken out of storage within a day. Where others tagged you, you remain only as a name they typed.

Encrypted backups of the server are kept for up to a year (a daily one for a week, a weekly one for five weeks, a monthly one for a year), so what you deleted is in them until they age out. They are used only to put Tapestry back after a disaster, and if one ever is, every account deleted since it was made is deleted again at once.

You can also ask for your account to be deleted by writing to Latent Sea at [contact email] from the email address you sign in with.

Before deleting, you can download a copy of everything Tapestry keeps about you from You, with Keep a copy.

Your rights

You may ask to see the data Tapestry keeps about you, to have it corrected, deleted, limited or handed to you to take elsewhere, and object to how it is used - by writing to [contact email]; you will have an answer within a month. Much of it you can do yourself: change anything in Tapestry, download everything with Keep a copy, or delete your account. If you are unhappy with how your data is handled, you may complain to the Agencia Española de Protección de Datos (aepd.es).

If you are named on Tapestry but are not on it

Members can name the people in their lives - with when they were born and died, if they know - tag them in memories, and add pictures of them. What a member puts on Tapestry is seen only by the people they choose, never by the public. Tapestry doesn't tell everyone named that they are, as it can't know who they are, but you have the same rights as a member: write to [contact email] to ask what is kept about you, to have it corrected or taken off, or to object to it, saying where you are named (whose life, which memory). The same goes for the relatives and heirs of someone who has died, for what is kept about them. You can also report something on Tapestry that you think is illegal or breaks the terms.

How long it is kept

For as long as your account is open; deleted, it is gone straight away, and from the backups within a year (above). The record of who looked at what is kept for as long as the account it is about. A report is kept until it is decided, and what was decided as long as the account it is about.

Children

Tapestry is not for children under 14: on joining, everyone confirms they are 14 or over.
